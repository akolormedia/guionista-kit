# Idea development

## Goal
Turn an idea (even a single sentence, a tone or a production constraint)
into a writable premise: logline, theme, genre, tone and production
constraints when they exist.

## Process
1. **Gather what already exists.** Ask about genre, format (feature,
   series, micro drama), production constraints (locations, budget,
   runtime) and any decision already made.
2. **Explore 3-5 premise directions** if the idea is still open. For each
   one: a one-line logline, why it works with the given constraints, and
   what makes it different from the others.
3. **Once one is chosen (or if it was already defined), define:**
   - Logline (one sentence: protagonist, what they want, what stands in
     the way)
   - Theme (what the story says about something human — without
     moralizing)
   - Genre and tone (comedy, drama, dramedy; light, bittersweet, etc.)
   - Production constraints (locations, cast, runtime)
   - What makes it culturally specific (when relevant) without falling
     into cliché
4. **Write the result to `bible/premise.md`** using this structure:
   ```markdown
   # Premise

   ## Logline
   ...

   ## Theme
   ...

   ## Genre and tone
   ...

   ## Production constraints
   ...

   ## Cultural context notes
   ...
   ```

## Example (Spanish)
Idea: "Una archivista encuentra cintas que contradicen la historia
oficial de su ciudad."
Logline: "Una archivista descubre cintas inéditas que contradicen la
historia oficial de su ciudad y debe decidir si las revela o protege el
único mundo que conoce."
Theme: "La memoria frente al olvido impuesto."

## Do not
- Do not move on to characters or structure until the premise is written
  in the bible — everything else rests on it.
- Do not invent production constraints the user did not give; ask about
  them when relevant (budget, locations).
