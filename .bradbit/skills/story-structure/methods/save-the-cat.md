# Save the Cat (Blake Snyder)

## What it is
A 15-beat sheet for feature films (typically 110 pages as a reference,
but scalable). Widely used in comedy and commercial Hollywood cinema for
its timing precision.

## Beats
1. **Opening image** — the protagonist's visual "before".
2. **Theme stated** — someone says, in passing, what the film is about.
3. **Setup** — world and character before the change; flaws are planted.
4. **Catalyst** — the event that breaks the status quo.
5. **Debate** — the protagonist doubts whether to act.
6. **Break into two** — they decide to act and enter a new world.
7. **B story** — a subplot (often relational) that carries the theme.
8. **Fun and games** — the "promise of the premise"; the most
   commercial/showy part.
9. **Midpoint** — false victory or false defeat; raise the stakes.
10. **Bad guys close in** — external and internal pressure increases.
11. **All is lost** — the lowest point; often with a "whiff of death"
    (literal or symbolic).
12. **Dark night of the soul** — the protagonist reaches emotional bottom.
13. **Break into three** — they find the solution, often by combining
    the A and B stories.
14. **Finale** — resolves the conflict, demonstrates the change.
15. **Final image** — mirror (or contrast) of the opening image.

## When it works best
Stories with a clear protagonist and a defined change arc; comedy,
thriller, commercial drama. Less natural for ensemble structures without
a single protagonist (it can be adapted by treating the group as "the
protagonist").
