# Eight-sequence structure

## What it is
It comes from the silent-film era (projection reels lasted ~10-15 minutes
each) and became a screenwriting teaching method (Frank Daniel, USC). It
divides the film into 8 blocks of ~10-15 minutes, each with its own
mini-arc (setup, complication, turn).

## Sequences (approximate mapping to 3 acts)
- **Sequence 1:** status quo + inciting incident
- **Sequence 2:** reaction to the inciting incident, decision to act
- **Sequence 3:** first attempts in the new world/plan
- **Sequence 4:** midpoint — raise the stakes
- **Sequence 5:** complications, the plan goes wrong
- **Sequence 6:** everything falls apart (end of act 2)
- **Sequence 7:** regroup, final plan
- **Sequence 8:** climax and resolution

## When it works best
Very practical for planning scene-by-scene outlines because each sequence
is the size of a manageable shooting/editing block. Good for low-budget
projects with few locations, because it encourages contained blocks of
continuous action.
